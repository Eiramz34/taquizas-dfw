Taquiza for a quinceañera in Dallas–Fort Worth

A taquiza is one of the most popular ways to feed 150–300 quince guests without paying a banquet hall's per-plate price. Here's how to plan it.

1. Check the venue first

Many halls only allow their own catering, or charge a fee for an outside caterer. Some don't allow open flames or a truck on site. Confirm in writing before you book a taquero. See DFW quince venues that allow outside catering.

2. Taquero on site or trays?

A taquero cooking fresh tortillas and al pastor at the party is part of the show, and needs space, power or propane, and the venue's OK. Trays are cheaper and work anywhere.

3. Plan the amount

Plan 3–4 tacos per adult if tacos are the meal. Use our tacos-per-person calculator.

4. Get it in writing

Hours of service, meats, sides, staff, deposit, and the date the final guest count is due.

Questions

Can a taquero cook inside a quinceañera venue?

It depends on the venue. Many allow a taquero with a griddle; fewer allow open flames, propane or a truck. Ask the venue for its rules in writing.

How far ahead should I book a taquero for a quince?

Popular Saturdays book early. Start asking 3–6 months before the date and confirm the final count about 1–2 weeks ahead, or when the caterer asks.
